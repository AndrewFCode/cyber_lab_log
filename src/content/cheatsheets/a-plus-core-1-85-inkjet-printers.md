> **In one line:** inkjets spray CMYK ink drops for cheap, quiet, high-res colour — the ink is pricey, fades and clogs, the print head is delicate (often built into the cartridge), and feed rollers pull the paper through.

*Companion to: Professor Messer A+ Core 1 (220-1201), Section 3, objective 3.8.* The full version is the Inkjet Printers class notes; the section overview is the Section 3 sheet.

## Inkjet at a glance

| Aspect | Detail |
| --- | --- |
| Method | Ink dispersion — drops from cartridge onto paper |
| Strengths | Inexpensive, quiet, high-resolution colour (graphics/photos) |
| Ink downsides | Expensive, proprietary, **fades over time**, **clogs easily** |
| Colours | **CMYK** = Cyan, Magenta, Yellow, **Key (black)** |
| Cartridges | Individual (per colour) or a single combined cartridge |
| Print head | **Combined** with cartridge (new head each swap) or **separate**; small and **delicate** |
| Feed rollers | Pull paper through; **clean them** if feed problems |
| Duplex | Some larger models; built-in or extra hardware — check maker |

## Beyond the clip (intro names these; body didn't cover them)

- **Carriage + belt:** the head rides a **carriage** moved across the page by a **belt**; worn/dirty belt or a binding carriage → **banding/misalignment/stalls**.
- **Calibration:** **print-head alignment** via a test page; **nozzle check** + **head-cleaning cycle** clears clogs (uses ink).
- **Drop mechanism:** **thermal** (bubble jet — HP/Canon) vs **piezoelectric** (Epson).
- **Ink type:** dye (vivid, fades faster) vs **pigment** (fade/water-resistant).

## 🔐 Security notes

- **Network inkjets are networked computers** (as in 3.7) — patch firmware, change defaults, segment; simple USB-only units carry far less risk.
- **Consumables = supply-chain/availability:** cartridge chips and vendor **firmware updates** can block third-party/refill ink (effective downtime); counterfeit cartridges bring quality/tamper risk. A clogged head or dirty rollers is itself downtime.

## Practice drills

<details>
<summary>1. What is inkjet output known for?</summary>

Inexpensive, quiet, high-resolution colour — popular for graphics and photos.
</details>

<details>
<summary>2. Three downsides of inkjet ink?</summary>

Expensive/proprietary, fades over time, and clogs easily.
</details>

<details>
<summary>3. What does CMYK stand for?</summary>

Cyan, Magenta, Yellow, Key (black).
</details>

<details>
<summary>4. Two print-head arrangements, and a benefit of the combined one?</summary>

Combined (head in the cartridge — fresh head each swap, simple/cheap) or separate head and cartridge.
</details>

<details>
<summary>5. What do feed rollers do, and the simple fix for feed problems?</summary>

Pull paper through the printer; clean the rollers if paper won't feed.
</details>

<details>
<summary>6. Is duplex guaranteed on an inkjet?</summary>

No — only some larger models; check the maker (may need extra hardware).
</details>

<details>
<summary>7. (Beyond the clip) One colour missing and banding — first checks?</summary>

Check ink levels, then run a nozzle check/head-cleaning cycle and alignment.
</details>

## Key takeaways

- **Inkjet = cheap, quiet, high-res colour;** ink is pricey, **fades** and **clogs**.
- **CMYK** (K = Key = black), individual or combined cartridges.
- **Print head** often built into the cartridge (fresh head each swap); it's **delicate**.
- **Clean feed rollers** for feed problems; **duplex** only on some models.
- **Beyond the clip:** carriage/belt (banding if worn), calibration = head alignment + nozzle clean.
