> **In one line:** the IT department deploys, administers and supports the technology a business runs on — and help desk is the front door to it.

## What "IT" actually means

- **Information Technology** — an umbrella term covering all technological work at an organisation.
- Every business has one, at varying sizes; smaller businesses often **outsource** it (MSPs).
- Responsibilities vary between organisations — job titles are not standardised.

## The seven core responsibilities

| Area | What it covers |
|---|---|
| **Infrastructure management** | Install, maintain and upgrade tech assets — computers, phones, networking gear, servers |
| **Tech support** | Help employees use technology: troubleshooting, training, setup, admin tasks |
| **Security** | Cyber *and* physical security. Sometimes its own department or a sub-department |
| **Data management** | Databases, data systems, access controls, auditing |
| **Identity & access management (IAM)** | Account creation, permissions, auditing, onboarding/offboarding |
| **Project management** | Planning upgrades and transitions; strategic planning for future demand |
| **Compliance & governance** | Meeting regulations and standards — privacy, data protection |

```
                          │        │        │                       │
                     IT Manager    │   Applications Mgr        Security team
                     ┌────┴────┐   │    ┌────┴────┐
              Network Engineer  │   │  Database   Application
                     │      Systems │   Admin      Analyst
              Network Admin     Admin│
                                  │  └──► Project Manager
                            IT Technician (L1–L3)   │
                                  │           IT Solutions Architect

```

**Two things worth noting:**
- Security often reports to a **CISO**, not the IT Director — a deliberate separation of duties.
- Help desk sits at the bottom of the support chain. That's the point: it's the **entry route** into everything above it.

## Roles at a glance

| Role | Level | Focus |
|---|---|---|
| **CTO** | Executive | Technological direction, leadership, policy, oversight |
| **IT Director** | Senior mgmt | All infrastructure, IT strategy, budget, team leadership |
| **IT Manager** | Management | Day-to-day ops, people management, high-level escalations, vendors, hiring |
| **Network Engineer** | IC | **Designs** and plans networks, installs/configures appliances, monitors, troubleshoots |
| **Network Administrator** | IC (junior) | **Maintains** and monitors the network — less design, more upkeep |
| **Systems Administrator** | IC | Servers and infrastructure (usually excl. network), patching, backups/recovery, top escalation tier |
| **IT Technician** | IC | Installs/configures hardware and software, user accounts, end-user support |
| **Applications Manager** | Management | Third-party software, vendor contracts, software projects, escalations |
| **Database Administrator** | IC | Database design, performance monitoring, data access controls, integrations |
| **Application Analyst** | IC | Deep support for one or a few specific applications; trains users, writes docs |
| **Project Manager** | — | Project planning, stakeholders, resources, budget, timelines, comms |
| **Solutions Architect** | — | Requirements analysis, high-level solution design, tech selection, systems integration |

**Engineer vs. Administrator:** engineer = *design and build*; administrator = *run and maintain*. Same pattern applies across most IT disciplines.

**Documentation appears in nearly every role.** It's not an afterthought — it's a core deliverable.

## Help desk: responsibilities

The **first point of contact** when a user needs technical support. Work arrives and is tracked through a **ticketing system**, and support is delivered by phone, remote software, or in person.

| Responsibility | Detail |
|---|---|
| **Front-line support** | Triage and resolve what you can; route what you can't |
| **Troubleshooting** | Hardware, software, networking, misc. Gather the information needed *before* diagnosing |
| **Password & account management** | Resets, unlocks, account creation/modification — following security procedure |
| **Customer service** | You're the face of IT, often to users who are already stressed |
| **Escalation** | Know the limit of your tier and where the ticket goes next |
| **Hardware** | Fixing and installing — and knowing hardware even in remote roles |
| **Documentation** | Runbooks, install guides, troubleshooting procedures — created and maintained by the team |

## The parts people underrate

**Soft skills.** Users are frequently dealing with something stressful when they reach you. Trust and relationships are built here, and they're what get you recommended for the next role.

**Knowing your tier.** Help desk and technician roles are commonly tiered **L1–L3**. Internal runbooks usually define what each tier handles and when to escalate. Escalating correctly is a skill; escalating too early or too late both cost you.

**Writing things down.** New issue solved → new documentation. This is often the most visible evidence that you're doing the job well.

## Key takeaways

- IT = deploy, administer, support the technology the business runs on.
- Titles vary wildly between organisations — read the responsibilities, not the job title.
- Help desk is front-line: tickets, troubleshooting, accounts, customer service, escalation, docs.
- Security may sit inside IT or report separately to a CISO.
- Documentation and soft skills are part of the technical job, not extras.
