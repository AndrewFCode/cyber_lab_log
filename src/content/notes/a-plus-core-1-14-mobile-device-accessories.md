## 1. Stylus

## 1.1 Why use one

Fingers work well for tapping and swiping, but they're imprecise. A __digital stylus__ works like a pen or pencil on the screen, giving much finer, more granular control — for handwriting, drawing, annotating documents or selecting small items.

## 1.2 How it works

__Feature__

__Detail__

Connection

Usually __Bluetooth__ — wireless

Pressure sensitivity

Pressing harder can produce thicker lines or darker shading

Buttons

Buttons on the stylus trigger extra functions, such as erase or right-click

Compatibility

The stylus must be __compatible with the device__. For example, an iPad needs an Apple Pencil (or a stylus designed for that iPad)

__Pairing the stylus made for the device gives the best connection and the most features.__ A generic stylus may work only as a basic pointer, without pressure sensitivity or buttons.

## 1.3 Worked example — "my stylus doesn't do anything"

1. __Check compatibility:__ is this stylus made for this tablet model? Styluses are often model-specific.
2. __Check pairing:__ is it connected in the tablet's Bluetooth settings?
3. __Check power:__ is the stylus charged?
4. __Test the features:__ if basic drawing works but pressure doesn't, the stylus is probably not fully compatible with this device.

## 2. Headsets

A __headset__ combines headphones and a microphone for hands-free calls — no holding the phone to your ear.

## 2.1 Wired headsets

__Connection__

__Notes__

USB

A common modern headset connection

TRRS (3.5 mm analog audio jack)

Lightning

For older iPhones without a headphone jack or USB-C, there are wired headsets with Lightning plugs

## 2.2 The TRRS connector

__TRRS__ stands for __tip-ring-ring-sleeve__ — the four electrical contacts on the plug. It's usually the __3.5 mm__ size, and is also called the __analog audio jack__.

   Plug, side view (front of the plug on the left):

   \[ TIP \]\[ RING 1 \]\[ RING 2 \]\[ SLEEVE \]======== cable
      ^        ^         ^         ^
   very end   middle    middle   nearest
              contact   contact  the back

__Note (beyond this lesson):__ the extra ring is what adds the __microphone__. A three-contact __TRS__ plug carries stereo audio only (left, right, ground). A TRRS headset in a TRS-only socket, or vice versa, may give sound with no microphone.

### 2.3 Wireless headsets

- __Connection:__ most connect over __Bluetooth__.
- __Power:__ they're __battery powered__. Someone who uses one all day may run the battery flat and need to switch to a __wired__ headset as a backup.
- __Benefit:__ no cable to tangle or snag, and freedom to move away from the device.

## 4. Webcams

- __Phones, tablets and many laptops__ already have a built-in camera.
- __Desktops, and laptops without one,__ can use an __external third-party webcam__, usually connected by __USB__.

__Once connected or enabled, the camera is available to applications__ for:

- capturing audio and video;
- video and audio __conferencing__.

## 5. Docking stations and port replicators

## 5.1 The problem they solve

An office laptop user who takes the laptop home each night must unplug everything — monitor, keyboard, mouse, network cable and so on — and plug it all back in every morning. That's slow and wears out connectors.

## 5.2 Docking station

A __docking station__ stays on the desk with all the cables permanently attached.

- __Morning:__ place the laptop in or on the dock and start working immediately — monitor, keyboard, mouse and network are all connected.
- __Evening:__ disconnect the laptop from the dock. Every cable stays in place.
- __Often provides extra interfaces__, and may even accept __full-size interface (expansion) cards__.
- __Typically made for a specific laptop__ (or family of laptops) from the manufacturer.

## 5.3 Port replicator

A __port replicator__ does a similar job with less:

- __Connects over USB__, so it works with almost __any__ laptop or desktop.
- __No support for adapter cards__, which makes it smaller and __more portable__.
- __Provides a range of ports__ — for example USB ports, audio, an __SD / microSD card reader__, and video outputs.

__Exam tip:__ a scenario mentioning __expansion or adapter cards__, or a model-specific dock on the desk, points to a __docking station__. A __portable, USB, works-with-anything__ device points to a __port replicator__.

__Note (beyond this lesson):__ many modern "docks" connect over a single USB-C or Thunderbolt cable and work across brands. The exam distinction above still holds: docking stations add more capability, and port replicators add ports.

## 6. Trackpads

## 6.1 Built-in and external

- __Built-in:__ in a confined space there may be no room for a mouse. The laptop's __trackpad__ (touchpad) — a large touch-sensitive area — moves the pointer with your fingers.
- __External:__ people who like trackpads can use one with a desktop. External trackpads are usually __battery powered__ and connect over __Bluetooth__.

## 6.2 Gestures

A trackpad can do things that are awkward with a mouse, using __multi-finger gestures__ — for example __expanding or contracting__ (zooming in and out) on the screen with a pinch.

## 6.3 Disabling the trackpad

When typing on a laptop, your palm can brush the trackpad and move the cursor or click by accident. Many laptops let you __turn the trackpad off with a function key__. On one example laptop, it's __Fn \+ F10__, marked with a trackpad icon, but the key varies by manufacturer.

## 6.4 Worked example — "the cursor jumps while I'm typing"

1. __Suspect accidental palm contact__ with the trackpad.
2. __If the user has an external mouse,__ disable the trackpad with its function key (check the icon on the F-keys), or in the OS touchpad settings.
3. __Also consider__ adjusting the touchpad's sensitivity or palm-rejection settings.
4. __Reverse check:__ if a user reports the trackpad "stopped working", check whether it was disabled with the function key.

## 9. Security perspective

- __Bluetooth accessories must be paired carefully.__ Pair only devices you recognise, and remove old pairings. Unpaired or unknown devices shouldn't be accepted.
- __Webcams and microphones__ in headsets and cameras can be hijacked by malware. Check which applications have permission to use them. A physical lens cover is the only control software can't override.
- __Docks and port replicators give instant access to networks and devices.__
	- A laptop dropped onto a dock joins the office network immediately.
	- Unknown docks, hubs or USB devices may not be what they seem — beyond this lesson, some malicious USB devices pretend to be keyboards and type commands.
	- Only use company-supplied or trusted accessories.
- __Card readers bring outside data in.__ An SD card is removable media, so apply the same caution as with USB sticks — scan it, and follow policy on removable media.
- __Lock the screen when you step away__ — docking and undocking is exactly when people leave laptops unattended.

# Summary

- __Stylus:__ pen-like precision, usually over Bluetooth, with pressure sensitivity and buttons. It must be compatible with the device (e.g. Apple Pencil with iPad).
- __Headsets:__
	- Wired over USB, TRRS or Lightning.
	- Wireless over Bluetooth, battery powered — keep a wired backup.
	- __TRRS = tip-ring-ring-sleeve__: the 3.5 mm analog audio jack, with two rings in the middle.
- __Speakers:__ Bluetooth and battery-powered, for better fidelity than the phone's own speakers.
- __Webcams:__ built into phones, tablets and many laptops; external ones connect over USB. Used for capture and conferencing.
- __Docking station vs port replicator:__ a docking station keeps the desk cabled for instant connect and disconnect, and may accept full-size interface cards. A port replicator is portable, uses USB, works with almost any computer, and doesn't take cards.
- __Trackpad:__ built-in, or external over Bluetooth. Supports multi-finger gestures, and can be disabled with a function key (e.g. Fn\+F10).
- __Drawing pad:__ an external digitizer with its own stylus, over Bluetooth or USB, for precise and artistic input.
